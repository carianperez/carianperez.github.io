case studies

I like building things from scratch and figuring out what actually needs to happen. Here are three problems I’ve owned, how I approached them, and what happened.

case study 1

pricing my bakery with a tool I built with Claude

the problem

As Roll Call grew, I needed to be crisp about my numbers and figure out whether this could ever be a real business. But I have a busy schedule and I’m not naturally a spreadsheet person, so bookkeeping had to be close to effortless or it wasn’t going to happen.

what I did

I built a custom expense tracker with Claude that covers weekly expenses, sell-through, profit, cost per roll, and ingredient inventory. Once I saw how expensive my rolls actually were to make, I added a tracker for what I was really earning per hour.

how AI fit in

I used Claude to build the tracker from scratch as an interactive tool, then kept iterating on it as I learned which numbers mattered. The pay-per-hour view came from a question the first version raised.

the result

The numbers showed I was severely undercharging. I raised prices from $5 to $7 per roll, and my pay went from $8 to $16 an hour. Nobody complained, I still sold out, and I just made more money.

what I’d do differently

Build it before launching and track from day one, so I start at the right price instead of finding out later.

case study 2

launching Roll Call without a playbook

the problem

After moving to Tampa, I wanted to start a small food business with no customer list and no playbook, and I had to decide what to sell.

what I did

I compared a matcha concept (I’d tried a small matcha pop-up in Seattle) against cinnamon rolls, and picked cinnamon rolls on purpose: lower startup costs, and better shelf stability so I wouldn’t waste product during the slow early days of a new business. Rolls can also be customized just as much as matcha drinks. I benchmarked local competitor pricing, picked target customers, and marketed directly to 5K races, local service businesses, firefighters, and hospitals. I set up Square Online for Saturday pickup orders.

Pop-up to pop-up, I changed the process, not just the product. I started handing out samples, and about 90% of samples turn into a sale. I also made my bake days more efficient as volume grew.

how AI fit in

I used AI for market and pricing research, analyzing my Square sales data, setting up the online store, and planning product copy and content.

the result

In about 3 months: a steady ~45 rolls a week, private event orders as large as 100 rolls, 3 sold-out pop-ups, and ~1,100 followers across platforms. I keep it small and high-margin on purpose.

what I’d do differently

Start making content earlier.

case study 3

getting a stalled feature back on track at Xbox

the problem

A flagship social feature on Xbox was 8 months behind its launch date, with 5 design and engineering teams blocked and no single source of truth.

what I did

I ramped up as the sole dedicated PM in under a month, worked directly with designers iterating on the UI, unblocked the 5 teams, and wrote the cross-org requirements doc that became the source of truth.

how AI fit in

I built Copilot and Claude into my daily workflow for spec drafting, debugging, and status reporting, including a workflow that read Teams meeting transcripts and updated Azure DevOps tasks and dates.

the result

Eliminated the 8-month launch slip.

what I’d do differently

Start asking questions earlier. The fastest unblocks came from asking the right people the right questions.
